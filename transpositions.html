Transpositions The 400 games

Where the games meet

The four hundred are four hundred openings. They are not four hundred games: play is greedy from the third move on, so two lines that ever reach the same position are the same game from there to the end. 67 of the 400 do that, in 25 groups. The other 333 never touch another game.

Only the joins are drawn. Everything between them is a single chain of positions with nothing to choose, and past the opening the corpus has no forks at all: a position has one best move, so a line can be joined but never split.

The only forks in the corpus are the 21 of its own construction: the start, where twenty first moves part, and each of those twenty, where twenty replies do. After that the 400 lines run without a choice until they meet. In every group below, each row is one game; the thin rail stands for the plies it played on its own, which are not to scale and are given as a count, and the bar is the stretch of the shared line it is on.

Four of the groups end in a shaded loop, and those 4 loops hold the only 64 pairs in the corpus where two games sit on the same position with a different number of plies left to play. Everywhere else a shared position means an identical future; in a loop it does not, because a game stops on the repetition of a position it has seen before, and a game that arrived later has not seen it yet.
